PDLC 3.0: Building the Agent-Native Opportunity Manager and Outcome Graph[a]
Updated: 2/19/26 + Figma Explorations
Context
The Product Development Loop
The best predictor of quality is the number of iterations. Every product team runs the same loop: build → ship → use → learn. The left side is now incredibly fast from 50 years of improvement: programming languages, agile, cloud, and now agentic coding. The distance from problem identified → solution ideated → code shipped is approaching zero. 
  

However the right side (use and learn) sits in the stone ages. More than 95% of data goes unlooked at. For every bug that's caught, hundreds of issues go undetected. Sensing opportunities and specifying plans is now the limited reagent for great product development.
The teams that have mastered this loop (ex: Anthropic, OpenAI, Meta, Netflix) run thousands of experiments and treat every interaction and release as signal. They're investing in proprietary systems to sense, prioritize, and close the loop at scale while everyone else iterates at a snail's pace. Amplitude has always played on the right side of the loop. We've built an exceptional data platform and insight generation machine. But we've stopped at the edge of value: we tell customers "here's what's happening" but have never led with "here's what to do about it." 


That gap, going from insight to action, is exactly where the next generation of great products will be built. Self-improving products don't just measure and report on what's trending. They proactively surface insights, prioritize what matters, recommend fixes, close the execution loop, then learn from what works. Amplitude can be the platform that enables this. By tracking what shipped and whether it worked, we build the proprietary data asset, the Outcome Graph, that makes self-improvement possible at scale.


A New Era of AI-Powered Builders
AI has shifted who can act on insights. A PM with Cursor can ship a feature. A designer with v0 can build a working prototype. An engineer with Claude can write their own PRD. A marketer with Claude Code can write their own automations. The walls between roles are collapsing into a new persona: the AI-powered builder - someone who can sense, decide, and directly act regardless of job title.
These builders can now ship ten things in the time it used to take to ship one. But their newest constraint isn't building, but sensing. They're drowning in dashboards, alerts, and fragmented signals across tools. They need ambient awareness of what's broken without actively looking for it. And as they orchestrate more agents and humans to tackle problems in parallel, they need systems that tell them what’s worth working on then whether or not their bets worked.
The path to self-improving products runs through AI-powered builders first. Before products can improve themselves, teams need to close the loop manually: see opportunities, act on them, measure results. As the Outcome Graph accumulates patterns and integrations mature, more of the loop can be automated. Builders become orchestrators. Orchestrators become supervisors. The tools we ship in 2026 are the foundation for the autonomous systems of 2027 and beyond.
Some examples of customers and AI community chatter on this issue:
* PM from Coinbase building their own coding agent harness and task management system
* Eng Lead from Coinbase saying 5% of all PRs merged coming from in-house coding agents now

* “Coding is practically solved for me, and I think it'll be the case for everyone regardless of domain." Not hedging. Not "in five years." Now. The title "software engineer" is going away. What replaces it: builder, PM, or "we keep it as a vestigial thing.””
* Competitors like Sentry are already building direct log -> issue generation -> PR generated via coding agent workstreams and seeing immense PMF on this use case
* Non-stop chatter like this about the SDLC being reinvented with coding agents


Why Amplitude Can Define PDLC 3.0
The first era of product development was waterfall: long cycles, big releases, infrequent feedback. PDLC 2.0 was agile: shorter cycles, continuous deployment, faster iteration. PDLC 3.0 is the agent-native loop: agents ambiently sensing opportunities, guiding prioritization, humans orchestrating agents on execution, then closing the feedback loop and improving future decisions across the product lifecycle.
Other tools own pieces of this workflow. GitHub owns code. Linear owns tickets. Figma owns design. But none of them deeply know what matters to users.
Amplitude owns that product context: [b]which features drive retention, where users drop off, what experiments moved metrics, and what users are asking for. We have the world's largest dataset on digital behavior: trillions of actions across thousands of products. That's exactly what builders need to decide what to work on next. And it's exactly what self-improving products need to close the loop. No one else has the right to win for being the product intelligence layer besides Amplitude.

[p0] The Opportunity Feed
Amplitude Opportunities is a feed that unifies insights from all sources then presents them as prioritized, actionable items. Every morning, builders ask: "What’s broken? What should I work on?" The Opportunity Feed has the answer. One place to see the product and growth opportunities that matter, ranked by estimated impact on metrics each org cares about, with clear next steps on what to do next.
In 2025, we explored showing the value of individual agents. Users had to create, trigger, subscribe, and manage insights themselves. In 2026, we flip the model: the Opportunity Manager sits at the center, unifying insights and actions across Amplitude’s platform and automatically identifying improvements from multiple sources and routing them to the right teams or destinations.
The Opportunity Feed is how Amplitude earns a place in the daily “builder” workflow. Not a passive[f] dashboard you check weekly, but a tool you open every morning to kickoff agents or teammates in one-click to work on the top problems identified across Amplitude[g]. This is the ambient and proactive sensing, deciding, and acting layer that makes self-improving products possible.
  

Opportunity Sources[h]
* Amplitude Agents: Dashboard, Session Replay, Feedback, Experiment, Taxonomy, and Global Agents generate opportunity recommendations directly.[i] 
   * Amplitude can also auto-create relevant agents based on your taxonomy and usage patterns with little to no manual steps required.
   * Global Agent may identify an insight or idea in a live chat, kickoff follow-up investigations in chat or via subagents, then convert those takeaways into opportunities to follow-up on.
* AI Feedback [j]& LLM Analytics[k]: Convert themes from customer feedback insights and LLM/agent trace clusters into clear opportunities on what to build or optimize.[l]
* Anomalies: Connect to chart anomalies, automatically fire when metrics deviate from expectations, then kickoff agents to investigate the root cause and plan how to fix it.
* Session Replays: Highlight dead clicks, rage clicks, or conversion drop-offs identified in Session Replays then assign an agent to investigate and recommend fixes.
* Experiments: Convert experiment findings into roll out or product improvements.
* PR Reviews / Webhooks: Surface opportunities based on pending code changes, then recommend improvements based on behavioral context or insights identified in the review process.
* User investigations: Human-initiated questions or goals that feed into the opportunity pipeline.
Key Opportunity Capabilities
* Impact Estimation: Each opportunity is scored against the metrics defined in your Amplitude taxonomy. Revenue, activation, retention, whatever matters to your business. The goal isn't false precision; it's giving builders enough signal to make informed tradeoffs.
* Collaborative Prioritization: V1 shows estimated impact and lets customers decide. Over time, we learn from behavior: when customers act on opportunities, the feed gets smarter. Customers can set explicit weights: "I care more about retention than conversion this quarter.[m][n]"
* Smarter Triggers: We need better mechanisms than the current naive daily and weekly schedules[o] on when new insights and opportunities get surfaced by Agents.
* Ambient Alerting: Notifications via email or Slack/Teams when opportunity status changes. You'll be alerted when your product breaks, when UX issues accumulate, when a launch underperforms.
[p0] The Recommendation Engine[p]
For each opportunity, we can build Product and/or Growth Agents to generate concrete recommendations informed by behavioral data, codebase context when available, and patterns from the Outcome Graph. And if a user dislikes the recommendation, they can override with their own context and preferences.[q][r][s]
Not just "checkout conversion dropped 12%" but "checkout conversion dropped 12% after Tuesday's deployment. The latency increase[t] in payment validation is the likely cause. Recommended fix: add caching to the validatePaymentMethod function. Expected impact: 8-15% conversion recovery based on similar patterns."
Moving into recommendations begins separating Amplitude from every other analytics tool. Our direct competition shows you data. Amplitude proactively analyzes it for accurate insights, reasons about what best levers to take, then works with you on executing it.
Key Capabilities[u]
* Product Agent: A specialized subagent that generates scoped recommendations, refined over time as we learn which recommendations get acted on and which get overridden.
* Codebase Connection (Phase 1): For customers who grant repo access, trace behavioral issues back to specific code paths, deployments, and commits. An insight is created identifying where users are struggling → codebase access shows where in the code the problem lives.
* Deploy Correlation (Phase 2): Know when deploys happen and correlate them with metric changes. No code access required.[v]
* Growth Agent (Phase 2): We can potentially build a specialized subagent that takes growth and conversion insights and ideates on relevant non-product/growth focused actions that can be taken (ex: orchestration, personalization, etc.)

[P2] Building the Outcome Graph
Every insight, opportunity, recommendation, action taken, and result becomes a proprietary dataset no competitor can replicate.
Most teams can ship quickly now. What actually limits velocity is learning. When a metric moves or an alert fires, teams need to understand what changed, why it mattered, and what to do next. That understanding typically lives in code and in the mental models engineers build over time. The Outcome Graph makes that understanding explicit, so teams and the AI systems they use can learn what matters and act faster.
This is the first time we can track the full loop: what recommendations get generated from an insight, what gets clicked or viewed, what gets acted on, how teams override agent recommendations (giving us labeled data on what plans they actually want), how the code and product evolves, and what metric movements result. Every signal feeds back to make recommendations smarter.
What we track
   * Insight to Opportunity: Which insights from across the product were relevant and important enough to create opportunities for (helping tune insight quality)
   * Recommendation Generation: Which insights trigger which recs, and how confident are we?
   * Engagement Signals: What gets viewed, clicked, dismissed? Which do teams care about?
   * Override Patterns: When humans edit or reject agent recommendations, what do they change? This is gold-standard labeled data for improving the Product Recommendation Agent.
   * Execution Tracking: What shipped, when, and how did the implementation differ from the rec?
   * Outcome Measurement: Did the metric improve? By how much vs. estimated?
What tracking outcomes enables
   * Confident recommendations: "Companies like yours saw 23% lift when they fixed this pattern."
   * Impact prediction: "Based on similar opportunities, we expect 15-25% improvement."
   * Amplitude taking credit: "Amplitude identified 23 opportunities that we acted on in the last quarter, leading to a X% increase in conversion and +$XXX increase in sales."
   * Peer benchmarks: "Your checkout conversion is in the bottom quartile for B2C e-commerce."
   * Compounding learning: Over time, this value compounds. New opportunities come with suggested fixes. New alerts come with meaningful runbooks. Launches come with checklists derived from real dependencies. Teams and their AI systems spend less time figuring out what matters and more time improving it.
