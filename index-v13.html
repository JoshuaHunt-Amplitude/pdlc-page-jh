Manifesto // Self-improving products
[ 2026 ]

The best predictor of quality
is the number of iterations.

It holds for code, for writing, for an AI model, for any system. The strategist John Boyd showed that the pilot who cycles their decision loop faster wins the dogfight; evolution's results are staggering only because it has had so long to iterate. Products are no different — to build a great one is to tighten the loop and fit in more iterations.

Every product development team runs the same loop: build, ship, use, learn.

The left side — build and ship — is now incredibly fast, after fifty years of progress: programming languages, agile, the internet, the cloud, and now AI coding. The distance between an idea and millions of people using it is going to zero. A single person can ship a product used by millions overnight.

The right side — use and learn — still sits in the stone ages. Understanding what people want is extraordinarily hard. It is the limiting reagent for great product development.

And nobody truly knows what people want — not companies, not markets, not the experts. The state of the art is to ask: interviews, studies, surveys, feedback. But people don't know what they want. They're good at naming problems and frustrations; the solution stays elusive. "If I had asked people what they wanted, they would have said faster horses."

So look at what people do. Actions speak louder than words. If someone keeps coming back, that's a signal. If they bring others, a better one. If they'd be upset to lose it, better still. This isn't about blindly chasing a metric — it's that behavior reveals intent. Automate and scale this half of the loop and you unlock the next step change in building great products.

We'll live in a world of self-improving products. The companies that win the next decade will master this loop — and Amplitude Wave gives every company the advantage only the world's best product teams have had: to sense, decide, and act.
